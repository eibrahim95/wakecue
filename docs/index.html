Wakecue: Lamp & Alarm

A bedside lamp and an alarm clock in one: an opal glass globe on a 3D-printed base. It sleeps on an 18650 cell almost all day, then, before any alarm it has been given — one of yours, or any of the five prayers — the globe fades from deep red through amber to warm white over the ramp you set, and the sound you chose plays at the time itself.

Roadmap

The goal: one bedside lamp, built and given to someone else. It wakes them on their own alarms or on any of the five prayers, with light and sound, and shows the day's prayers for their place. It asks for nothing but a phone: no PC, no password compiled in, no reflashing. This section is the plan; the rest of this page and the other pages are reference — how it is wired, what it costs, how it looks.

Where the build stands is kept in one place only: STATUS.md at the root of the repo — the current move, what has been seen on the board, what is owned and what is still to buy. These pages do not repeat it.

1 · It keeps its own time · first

What the owner does: a first boot with nothing stored raises Wakecue-XXXX, the phone opens the page the lamp serves, and they pick the network, the time zone and the place. The lamp keeps all three, sets its clock from the network whenever the clock cannot be trusted, and computes the five times for that place. The wall clock stops being Egypt's and becomes whatever the block says.

Gate: with no cell fitted and the lamp unplugged, a phone alone brings the face to the right local time and the right five times for a place that is not Cairo; a second unplug keeps all of it.

Trip-wire, written down before starting: the radio needs allocation and an executor inside the lamp. If a real phone cannot get an address from our own DHCP server within a couple of days of bench work, that half moves to ESP-IDF and gets re-planned rather than fought.

2 · It wakes you · after 1

The ring on GPIO10 through RMT with the ramp from core::ramp, the AO3400 rail switch on GPIO5, and the MP3 card on GPIO20/21 playing the adhan from its own card. The alarm that already takes the panel gets its sound and its light from the same call.

Gate: at its minute an alarm runs the ramp and plays the sound it carries, and any key or touch stops it; a prayer does the same with its own track. Two alarms on one morning ring like two alarms, not like one alarm and one special case.

Checks on the way:

  • The ring ramps red to warm white in a dark room at 4.2 V and at 3.2–3.5 V (the LM2596 from a USB charger stands in for an empty cell; this is below the ring's 3.5 V datasheet minimum). Ring current measured.
  • Track 1 plays at a usable volume at 4.2 V and at 3.2–3.5 V, and the frame commands work on the real board (the listing never names the player's chip).
  • No chip reset when the rail switches on (slow gate: 1 kΩ), nor when the speaker starts with the ring lit.
  • Rail-off draw back at the sleep baseline. If it is not, current is leaking through the ring or player pins while their ground is open, and the switch moves to the high side (the AO3401s) before anything else.
  • Fallbacks, only if needed: a 1N5819WF in series with the ring supply if the first pixel glitches at 4.2 V; the MT3608 5 V boost for the ring and player if colours or sound fail below about 3.5 V (that route also needs a 3.3 → 5 V level shifter from elsewhere).

3 · It ships · after 2

The difference between a bench and a lamp: a CR2032 with the DS3231 charge resistor lifted, so an unplug no longer costs the time; a factory reset (hold Back at boot) so the lamp can be handed on; the sleep current measured instead of estimated; the panel's hidden edge measured; and everything soldered onto the 5×7 cm board in the base.

Gate: the lamp on the shelf, unplugged for a day, still showing the right time; a measured sleep current that matches the battery table; the board in the base, no breadboard.

Checks on the way:

  • Power LEDs off the SuperMini and the DS3231, and the DS3231 charge resistor lifted, before a cell goes in (see the warning in the build log).
  • Whole-board sleep current under 0.5 mA on the 2 mA range, with a jumper across the meter opened only once the chip is asleep; measured on the board alone, then with the display, expander and INA219. The ten-minute average including the minute wakes replaces the calculator default.
  • The INA219 fitted on the same I²C wires (0x40, no clash with 0x20, 0x57, 0x68), no power LED and no pull-ups to the wrong voltage, and a plausible cell voltage with the ring and player off.
  • The cell charges through the TP4056 while the board runs; charge termination judged with the lamp asleep; the cell voltage at which the chip resets written down.
  • The globe bought and its neck measured (outside diameter, length, lip, weight); the STLs regenerated with python base.py round <neck mm> and the Hawk quote repeated.
  • Printed and assembled: every part fits, the lamp does not tip, the speaker is audible through the 0.6 cm gap, the lid closes on all four screws, and each button cap moves freely and clicks its switch.

How we work

  • One move at a time. A move ends when its gate has passed on the board — not when the code lands and not when it builds.
  • Code time is not your time. While a move is being built I write it, test it on the PC and commit; you are pulled in at the gate, or when a decision needs your hands.
  • One bench session per gate, written as numbered steps, with nothing else in flight.
  • The record moves with the gate. When you report one, the build log and STATUS.md change in the same commit.

Overview

Reading the sizes: all sizes are in centimetres. Ø means diameter, the width of a circle measured straight across its middle, so “Ø16 cm” is a circle 16 cm wide. For scale: the whole lamp is about 16 cm wide and 20 cm tall, roughly the width of an A4 sheet (21 cm) in height, and the globe is a bit bigger than a grapefruit.

Before you order the globe and the print
  • Buy the globe first and measure its neck (outside diameter, length, any lip). The printed seat is sized from it.
  • Pick the base colour (black PLA is the default; matte black PLA-CF costs about 155 EGP more).
  • Compare all five generated prayer times with your mosque’s timetable and note any constant difference for each; every prayer has its own offset.

How it works

An ESP32 that stays awake burns tens of milliamps and has no accurate clock. So the jobs are split: a DS3231 clock (about a minute of drift per year, per its spec) keeps time and pulls its INT line low when an event is due, while the ESP32-C3 sleeps until that line or a touch wakes it. It runs the event, programs the next one into the clock, and goes back to sleep. The ring and the MP3 board share one switched ground, so neither draws anything while it sleeps.

The daily cycle

What you can schedule

The schedule is a short list of rules. Each rule says which days, when and what to do, and you can have as many rules as you like.

When
  • A prayer ± minutes: Prayer(Fajr, 0) is Fajr itself, Prayer(Asr, -20) 20 minutes before Asr, Prayer(Isha, 10) 10 minutes after Isha. Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib and Isha are computed on the boot from the stored place, so they follow wherever the lamp is told it sits.
  • A fixed time in Egypt time, for example 07:30. Daylight saving is handled automatically.
Which days
  • Any mix of the week: every day, Sunday–Thursday, Friday only, and so on.
ActionWhat it does
Sunrise { ramp_min }The colour sunrise: deep red → amber → warm white over N minutes, finishing exactly at the rule’s time
Play { track, volume, fade_in_s }Plays /mp3/000N.mp3 from the microSD card, fading the volume up over N seconds
LampOn / LampOffTurns the lamp on or off at that time
Alarm { sound }The lamp's own alarm. Today the panel flashes at full brightness and the tone index rides with the rule, so the ring and the card get it when they are wired.
Example schedule
const RULES: [Rule; 6] = [
    // Every day: each prayer's own adhan (prayer n plays track n)
    Rule { days: ALL_DAYS,    when: When::Prayer(Prayer::Dhuhr, 0),   action: Action::Play { track: 2, volume: 22, fade_in_s: 30 } },
    Rule { days: ALL_DAYS,    when: When::Prayer(Prayer::Maghrib, 0), action: Action::Play { track: 4, volume: 22, fade_in_s: 30 } },
    // Lamp on 10 minutes before Asr, off 30 minutes after Isha
    Rule { days: ALL_DAYS,    when: When::Prayer(Prayer::Asr, -10),   action: Action::LampOn },
    Rule { days: ALL_DAYS,    when: When::Prayer(Prayer::Isha, 30),   action: Action::LampOff },
    // Sunday–Thursday: gentle wake-up at 07:30 with track 6
    Rule { days: 0b0001_1111, when: When::Local(7*60 + 30), action: Action::Sunrise { ramp_min: 10 } },
    Rule { days: 0b0001_1111, when: When::Local(7*60 + 30), action: Action::Play { track: 6, volume: 15, fade_in_s: 60 } },
];
// days: bit 0 = Sunday … bit 6 = Saturday

Buttons and menu

The touch pad and any of the four buttons wake the lamp: the expander's interrupt shares the clock's wake line, so a press is as good as a tap. The buttons sit in a row under the screen and are read through the PCF8574 while the lamp is awake; ten seconds after the last press it goes back to sleep. A tap shows the standby face and lights the globe at 5% so you can read it. A hold of one second turns the lamp on or off, and any touch stops an alarm that is ringing. OK on the standby face opens the menu; Back steps out one level and, on the standby face, does nothing.
